Indonesia's Independence

How did Indonesia become a free country? Walk through the key moments that led to 17 August 1945.

17 August 1945
Hari Kemerdekaan Indonesia

The story

For 350 years, Indonesia was ruled by the Netherlands (called "Dutch East Indies"). Then in 1942, Japan took over during World War II. When Japan was about to lose the war in August 1945, Indonesian leaders saw their chance.

Timeline

7 August 1945

BPUPKI finishes its work

A committee finished drafting the Pancasila philosophy and the constitution that would guide the new nation.

14 August 1945

Soekarno and Hatta return from Saigon

Japan surrendered to the Allies on 15 August. Indonesian youth leaders urged Soekarno to declare independence immediately — before the Dutch could return.

16 August 1945

The Rengasdengklok incident

Youth leaders took Soekarno and Hatta to a small town called Rengasdengklok to pressure them to declare independence without waiting for Japan's permission.

17 August 1945, 10:00 AM

📢 The Proclamation

At Soekarno's house on Jalan Pegangsaan Timur No. 56, Jakarta, Soekarno read the short proclamation: "We, the people of Indonesia, hereby declare our independence." The red-and-white flag was raised for the first time.

1945–1949

War of Independence

The Dutch tried to take back control. For 4 years, Indonesians fought back — with weapons and with diplomacy. Many heroes gave their lives.

27 December 1949

🇳🇱 Dutch recognize Indonesia

The Netherlands officially recognized Indonesia's sovereignty. The new nation joined the United Nations in 1950.

📜 The Proclamation text

"Proklamasi kami, bangsa Indonesia. Hal ini kami sampaikan dengan sesungguhnya. Perihal pemindahan kekuasaan dan lain-lain dilaksanakan dengan cara sederhana dan dalam tempo sesingkat-singkatnya."

Jakarta, 17 Agustus 1945 — Soekarno / Hatta

Did you know?

  • The flag was sewn by Fatmawati, Soekarno's wife, from a white bedsheet and red dye.
  • August 17th is celebrated every year as Independence Day — with flag-raising ceremonies, neighborhood games, and decorations.
  • The original proclamation text was typed by Sayuti Melik on a typewriter.
  • Indonesia's national motto is "Bhinneka Tunggal Ika" — "Unity in Diversity" — meaning many cultures, one nation.
